User guide

PPL Progress

A study dashboard for the FAA Private Pilot Knowledge Test. This guide walks through every workflow in the app, step by step, with screens from the demo. The default goal is 85% on the 60-question test (passing is 70%).

01Start here

The app is a single HTML file that runs in your browser. There is no install, no server and no account.

  • Personal version (index.html): your real history, with optional sync between devices.
  • Demo (demo.html): a year of fictional data, a sample plan and a guided tour. The demo uses separate storage and never touches your data.
  • Tabs: Dashboard (day to day), Plan (the calendar of your plans), Practice tests and Statistics.
  • At the top: the sync chip, the button that opens this guide, the language flags (English, Portuguese, Japanese) and the gear for Settings.

Everything is saved in your browser. To take your data to another device, use sync or the backup in Settings.

02Dashboard

A snapshot of right now: where you are in the question bank, what to study today and how each topic's memory is holding up.

Question bank progress

The bar splits the 1,058 questions across the Kanban lists. Each color is a list, and the legend shows the questions and percentage in each one.

Countdown, practice tests and volume

  • Exam countdown: the days until the exam. Change the date in the field right below it or in Settings.
  • Practice tests: the last score, the best, the average of the last 3 and when the next test is due.
  • This week: questions done this week, with the last 12 weeks. The ⤢ icon opens the full volume view, with intervals from 3 days to all time.

Kanban and sessions

One card per topic, in 5 lists: To Do, Doing, Not Comfortable, Very Comfortable and To Review. The number on each card is your last score on that topic.

Move a card: drag it with the mouse, or press and hold it on a touch screen. The other cards slide aside to open a gap, and the card lands exactly where you drop it: in another list or in a new place in the same list. The order is saved and syncs. Every change shows a notice with Undo.

Log a session:

  1. Click the card's name (or select it and press Enter).
  2. Choose Studied (first time), Reviewed (a review) or Status only (just change the list).
  3. Type the session's score. Leave it blank if it is still in progress: the session counts as started but doesn't feed the memory model.
  4. Adjust the list and the date if needed, then click Save.

Your own cards: + Add a card at the end of a list adds a task of your own, such as "Review the theory" or "Radio calls: ground". Enter adds it and keeps the box open for the next one; Esc closes it. Your cards drag like any card; click one to rename or delete it. They are only reminders: they never count in the progress bar, the statistics or the forecast.

Fix just the score: click the colored number on the card, type and press Enter. The score of the last session is updated.

Calendar

Each day shows what was studied, reviewed and any practice tests. The WK column marks the weeks with study, and the number below it is the current streak. Click a day to see its summary.

Forecast, spaced repetition and today's plan

  • Exam forecast: your predicted exam score, with the pass and goal lines. Click it to see the 3 topics that cost you the most questions.
  • Spaced repetition: the memory curve of one topic. Every 3 seconds it moves through the topics in today's plan, and it stops while you hover over it. Hovering over a card shows that card's curve.
  • Today's plan: what to study today. The tag next to the title says where it comes from: Daily plan, Weekly plan or the energy of the suggestion. The circle marks an item as done.

Quick log

  1. Press ⌘K (Mac) or Ctrl K, or / outside a text field.
  2. Type part of the topic's name.
  3. Enter logs it as studied, ⇧ Enter as reviewed and ⌥ Enter opens the card. Esc closes.

03Plans

The Plan tab is your study calendar: you book blocks of time, the app puts the right topics in them, and you adjust before saving.

Week view

  • ‹ › change the week and Today goes back to the current one.
  • On the right: the time planned, the time done and how many blocks were missed this week.
  • Each block shows the time, the topic and the questions. The color tells the reason: review, weak topic, no data, new material, practice test, error review or your own activity. Clicking a block opens that topic's log.
  • A day that comes from a daily plan gets a D mark.
  • When today has no active plan, Today's energy appears: low, medium or high changes the size and order of today's suggestion.
  • From Friday on, if next week has no plan, a notice suggests planning it.

Where today's plan comes from: first the active daily plan for the date, otherwise the active weekly plan for the week, otherwise the suggestion from your statistics.

Create a plan

Click + New plan. There are 4 steps.

  1. Type. Choose Daily plan (one date) or Weekly plan (Monday to Sunday). Pick the date or week and, if you like, give it a name.
  2. Time blocks. Click and drag down on a day to book time, in 15-minute steps. The drag stays on the day where it started, even if the mouse slips onto another, and stops when you let go. Dragging over booked time removes it. On touch: tap the start, then the end. Use Undo / Redo, Clear and Copy … to the other days.
  3. Topics. The app suggests what to study in each block. Review and adjust (see below).
  4. Review. See how the week will look and click Save & activate or Save without activating.

How topics are chosen. Each block is filled to protect as many questions as possible on exam day, given the time each question takes (your speed, the topic's difficulty and the time to review mistakes). Inside a block, the hardest topic, or the one longest without review, comes first. To avoid switching subjects, a block holds at most one topic per ~45 minutes (deep work, configurable). In a weekly plan, each day assumes the earlier days were done: a Monday review doesn't come back on Tuesday.

Error review. Right after each topic comes an Error review of that topic's mistakes, the way the question bank shows the misses of a category as soon as you finish it. The block's review time is sized to the mistakes expected in it: questions × (1 − chance of a right answer) × difficulty × minutes per mistake, rounded to 5, at least 10 minutes and at most a quarter of the block, and none when the block has fewer than 3 expected mistakes. That time is then split among the block's topics in proportion to each one's expected mistakes, in 5-minute steps; a topic whose share is under 5 minutes gets no review. Each review row shows its topic's expected mistakes, for example "8 expected misses". To keep everything in the same block, the app shortens the easiest topic first. Only when a practice test or a topic that can't be shortened fills the whole block does the review move, as one item, to the start of the next block. If 20 minutes or more are left over, another topic is added, and no topic gets less than 10 minutes.

Editing in step 3:

  • Drag a card to move it: on a computer, grab it anywhere; on a phone or tablet, use its grip ⠿. The other cards open a gap where it will land, between any two cards of the same day, and the times are laid out again when you let go. A topic moves together with its error review.
  • Click or tap a card to edit it: change the questions, pick another category or the practice test, or type your own name (for example "Theory: weather fronts"). × removes it, and removing a topic also removes its error review.
  • + add in free time: add a topic, the practice test, or Your own activity, with a name and minutes, such as "Simulator" or "Review the material".
  • Each day's energy (low, medium, high) redoes that day's suggestion. Suggest again redoes everything.

Replacing an active plan. A daily plan over an active weekly plan asks whether to replace just that day. Another weekly plan for the same week asks whether to replace the week. Cancel saves nothing.

My plans

The My plans tab lists every plan, active ones first. Each has:

  • View: opens the plan read-only.
  • Edit: opens it at the time blocks, with the saved topics.
  • Activate / Deactivate: turns it on or off, asking before replacing another active plan.
  • Copy to next week (weekly): starts a new plan with the same blocks one week later and suggests new topics.
  • ×: deletes it, with undo.

Sync with Kanban

The Kanban never changes on its own. Sync with Kanban shows what would change for the Kanban to match today's plan:

  • Topics in today's plan that sit in another list move to Doing.
  • Topics in Doing that aren't in the plan go back to the list they came from, or to To Do.
  • Your own activities in today's plan (for example "Radio calls practice") become cards in Doing; if you already have a card with that name, it moves to Doing instead. Cards you make on the Kanban never go into the plan.

Uncheck anything you don't want and click OK. A single Undo reverts everything.

Done and missed

A topic in the plan is marked done automatically when you log a session for it on that day. You tick your own activities in the circle. An item from a past day that wasn't done shows as missed, and that topic gets priority in the next suggestion. The plan doesn't rearrange itself.

04Practice tests

FAA practice tests always have 60 questions. The tab shows the numbers, the trend and the full list.

  1. Click Log a practice test (or + Log practice test).
  2. Type the score. Leave it blank if the test is in progress.
  3. Adjust the date and save. To fix it later, use Edit in the list.
  • Next test: the panel at the top says when the next one is due, suggested every N days (and sooner if you go days without studying). + Add to today's plan puts a practice test in today's plan. With an active plan, it goes into the first free slot that fits.
  • Trend: the line through your results, with the projection to the exam and the date you reach your goal.
  • Tests left in progress for days show a notice, so you can complete or delete them.

05Statistics

Question bank

  • Question-bank model: the expected score today, the chance to pass, the chance to reach your goal and the score on exam day if you stop reviewing. The chart has intervals from 3 days to all time.
  • Where you lose the most questions: the topics that cost you the most questions on a 60-question test.
  • Needs recall: every 3 seconds, the curve of a topic in today's plan, from most to least urgent. The dots jump to a topic.
  • Heat map by topic: one square per topic, with the chance of a right answer today, from coldest to hottest. Click the card to see the history by day, week or month, with filters and hover.
  • Study volume: always open, with the total, the comparison with the previous period and the intervals.

Topic cards: below is one card per tracked topic, with the current memory, the stability, the next review, the sessions, the last score and the curve. Filter by Due or by list, pick the chart interval and use Log a review.

Practice tests

The forecast from practice tests (weighted average of the latest ones), the trend, the score histogram, the full forecast (chance to pass and to reach your goal, tests passed, consistency) and the model's accuracy compared with your real tests.

07Sync and your data

Sync between devices

In the personal version, the sync chip (or Manage sync… in Settings) turns on syncing through a private GitHub Gist.

  1. Create a GitHub token with only the "gist" permission.
  2. Paste the token and click Connect. Do this once on each device, with the same token.
  3. Done: every change syncs on its own. Sessions, tests and plans made offline on both devices are merged, and deleted items don't come back.

The token stays only in this browser. It never goes into Backup JSON, Save HTML or the Gist. Don't share the token with anyone.

Sync is optional. Without it, your data is still saved in this browser on its own.

Backup and copy

  • Backup JSON: downloads all your data, including plans and settings.
  • Import: restores a backup (an invalid file is refused without changing anything).
  • Save HTML: downloads a copy of the app with your data already inside.

08On your phone

Everything works on phones and tablets, with these differences:

  • The Kanban scrolls sideways, one list at a time, with dots below.
  • The spaced-repetition preview has a topic picker, and tapping a plan item shows its curve.
  • The Plan tab shows one day at a time, picked from the day strip. Energy becomes a simple menu.
  • In time blocks, tap the start and then the end. Dialogs open from the bottom.
  • Charts accept tap and drag to read values without locking page scrolling.
  • The book icon at the top opens this guide.

09How the numbers are calculated

A single statistics engine feeds every screen, so the same number reads the same everywhere. The "How it is calculated" sections in the app have the full formulas.

memory R(t) = 1 / (1 + t / 9S) S = stability, grows after good sessions next review when R falls to the review point (90% → S days) right answer p = score × R + 1/3 × (1 − R) (forgetting = guessing among 3) bank model μ = Σ (topic questions / 1,058) × p forecast bank μ blended with the last 3 practice tests (10-day half-life) pass / goal beta-binomial over 60 questions: P(X ≥ 42) and P(X ≥ 51) plan order (1 − p on exam day) × urgency ÷ minutes per question blocks knapsack per block, hardest first, 1 topic per ~45 min error review expected mistakes = Σ questions × (1 − p) × difficulty block minutes = mistakes × min per mistake (≥ 10, ≤ ¼ of the block, only if ≥ 3 mistakes) each topic's review = block minutes × its mistakes ÷ the block's mistakes (5-min steps)

In the practice-test trend, the projection slows down after the last test instead of running up to 100%.

10The science behind it

What each statistic means, the formula behind it, and the research it comes from. You don't need any of this to use the app; it is here so you can trust (or question) the numbers.

Memory decays, and reviews slow the decay

Right after a session you remember almost everything; then recall drops fast and keeps dropping more slowly. This is the forgetting curve, first measured by Ebbinghaus in 1885. Later work showed it is better described by a power law than by a plain exponential. The app uses the power-law curve from the FSRS scheduler:

R(t) = 1 / (1 + t / 9S) R recall: the chance you still remember the topic (0 to 1) t days since your last session on the topic S stability: how many days it takes for R to fall to 90%

Stability is the heart of it. Each good session increases S, so the curve gets flatter and the next review can wait longer. That is the spacing effect: the same study time, spread out, gives much longer-lasting memory than cramming. Each session is also a test, and recalling an answer strengthens memory more than rereading it (the testing effect, or retrieval practice).

When to review. A topic is due when R falls to the review point (90% by default, in Settings). Solving R = 0.9 gives the interval:

interval = 9S × (1 / review point − 1) at 90% → interval = S days

A higher review point means shorter intervals and more reviews; a lower one means fewer reviews and more forgetting in between.

From memory to an expected score

FAA questions have 3 options. If you remember a topic you answer at your last score's level; if you have forgotten it, you still get about 1 in 3 right by guessing. So the chance of a right answer on a topic is a mix of the two:

p = score × R + 1/3 × (1 − R)

The test draws its 60 questions roughly in proportion to how many questions each topic has in the bank, so the expected score is the weighted average:

μ = Σ (questions in topic / 1,058) × p

Chance to pass. The number of right answers out of 60 follows a binomial distribution if every question has the same chance μ. But μ is itself an estimate, and the app knows how far off it has been: its error against your real practice tests. So it uses a beta-binomial distribution, which spreads the chances by that uncertainty instead of pretending μ is exact. Passing is 42 of 60 (70%), and the default goal is 51 of 60 (85%):

chance to pass = P(X ≥ 42) chance of the goal = P(X ≥ 51) X ~ BetaBinomial(60, μ)

"If you stop reviewing" runs the same calculation on exam day, letting every curve decay with no more sessions. The gap between today's score and that line is what your reviews are protecting.

Practice tests and the blended forecast

Practice tests measure the whole thing directly, but each one is noisy. The forecast averages your last 3 tests with exponential decay weights: a test loses half its weight every 10 days (the half-life, in Settings). It then blends that average with the question-bank model μ, so a single good or bad day doesn't swing the forecast.

weight of a test = 0.5 ^ (age in days / half-life)

The trend is a least-squares line through your scores. Its projection slows down after the last test instead of running to 100%, because scores level off as you approach your ceiling. The model's accuracy chart compares what the bank model predicted on each test day with the score you actually got.

How the plan decides what you study

Each topic gets a priority: how many points it can still give you on exam day, how urgent its review is, and how long its questions take.

priority = (1 − p on exam day) × urgency ÷ minutes per question

Filling a block is a knapsack problem: choose the topics whose total time fits the block and whose total value (questions protected) is as large as possible. The app solves it exactly for each block, then puts the hardest topic first, when your attention is freshest.

Error review. Getting a question wrong and then studying why is one of the most effective ways to learn, especially when the feedback comes soon after the mistake. That is why each topic is followed right away by a review of its own mistakes, sized in proportion to the mistakes you are expected to make on it, inside the same block rather than another day.

Deep work and planning (Cal Newport)

The plan follows the ideas in Cal Newport's Deep Work and his time-block planning method:

  • Time-block planning. Give every minute of a study session a job before it starts. The Plan tab is a time-block planner: you book blocks first, then decide what goes in each one.
  • Deep work over switching. Each switch between subjects leaves "attention residue": part of your mind stays on the previous task (Leroy, 2009). The deep-work limit keeps at most one topic per ~45 minutes of a block.
  • Hardest work first. The most demanding topic takes the start of each block, and your energy for the day changes the size and order of the suggestion.
  • Planned versus done. The week shows the time you planned, the time you did and the blocks you missed. Missed topics get priority next time, but the plan never rewrites itself: you decide.
  • Weekly planning. From Friday on, if next week has no plan, the app nudges you to plan it, so the week starts with intention instead of improvisation.
  • You stay in control. Nothing moves on the Kanban until you approve it with Sync with Kanban.

Practicing the topics that cost you the most, with a clear goal per session and fast feedback, is also the core of deliberate practice (Ericsson et al., 1993).
